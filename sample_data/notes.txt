Chapter 1.1 Photosynthesis
Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants make glucose from carbon dioxide and water using sunlight. Chlorophyll is the green pigment in chloroplasts that absorbs light energy. In the light reaction, water molecules are split and oxygen is released as a by-product. The energy captured in the light reaction is later used to reduce carbon dioxide into carbohydrates. Stomata are tiny pores on the leaf surface through which carbon dioxide enters the plant.
Chapter 1.2 Respiration
Respiration is the process that releases energy from glucose inside living cells. Aerobic respiration takes place in the presence of oxygen and produces carbon dioxide, water and a large amount of energy. Anaerobic respiration occurs without oxygen and releases much less energy from each glucose molecule. In yeast, anaerobic respiration produces ethanol and carbon dioxide. The energy released during respiration is stored in molecules of ATP.
Chapter 1.3 Digestion
Digestion is the breakdown of large food molecules into smaller molecules that the body can absorb. Salivary amylase in the mouth begins the digestion of starch into simple sugar. The stomach releases hydrochloric acid and the enzyme pepsin, which begins the digestion of proteins. The small intestine is the main site of complete digestion and absorption of food. Finger-like villi increase the surface area of the small intestine for absorption.
Chapter 2.1 Circulation
The human heart has four chambers that keep oxygen-rich blood separate from oxygen-poor blood. Arteries carry blood away from the heart and have thick elastic walls. Veins carry blood back to the heart and contain valves that prevent backward flow. Capillaries are very thin vessels where materials are exchanged between blood and surrounding cells.
Chapter 2.2 Excretion
Excretion is the removal of harmful metabolic wastes from the body. The kidneys filter blood and form urine, which contains urea and excess water. Each kidney contains a very large number of filtering units called nephrons. Useful substances such as glucose and much of the water are reabsorbed as the filtrate flows along the nephron.
